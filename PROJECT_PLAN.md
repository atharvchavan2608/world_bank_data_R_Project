# Trust in Courts, Trust in Credit?

## Research question and estimand

Among economies with a recent World Bank Enterprise Survey, how does the share of eligible firms holding a bank loan or credit line vary with confidence in court impartiality and exposure to bribery? How does it vary with the legal origin of company or commercial law? Estimate associations for an equally weighted economy. Enterprise Survey weights already enter the national indicators. The regression does not weight economies by population or by the number of interviewed firms.

Banks may lend more when they expect enforceable contracts. Bribe requests may raise costs or discourage formal activity. Institutional quality and credit depth may also share antecedents such as income, state capacity and financial development. Perceptions can respond to economic conditions and firms' interactions with banks and courts. The report will distinguish credit holding from applications, approval, terms and unmet need.

## Model sequence and inference

1. State the population regression function, outcome, regressor units, stochastic disturbance and CLRM assumptions. Define OLS as a linear projection and distinguish its orthogonality condition from conditional mean independence and the stronger assumptions needed for classical tests. Begin with court doubt alone, with a negative directional expectation and a two-sided test.
2. Add bribery incidence, log of average workers and the common-law dummy in stages. Refit the simple model on the multivariate model's exact sample and report both fits. Keep FIN20, FIN11, DISP1 and DISP2 for sensitivity because demand, the collateral terms of observed loans and dispute selection can be jointly determined with credit. Compare CRIME9 as an alternative for court doubt and show the full pitch specification on a common sample if the overlap supports it. Treat collinearity using theory, coefficient stability and VIF rather than a mechanical selection rule.
3. Test the court, bribery and legal-origin coefficients individually. Report t statistics, p-values, 95% confidence intervals, overall and institutional-block F tests, and both R-squared measures. For the signed court versus bribery comparison, test whether the difference between the two slope-times-sample-SD values is zero, using HC3 covariance on the same sample. Also report each variable's partial R-squared. A comparison of predictive associations cannot rank reforms. Use percentage-point effects for a ten-point change in each survey measure and a doubling of average firm size.
4. Compute studentized Breusch–Pagan, VIF, residual and influence diagnostics and tests of residual ordering. Report Durbin–Watson under economy-code and an alternate order, with permutation context. Use HC3 as primary uncertainty under independent but heteroskedastic economy errors. Show conventional inference alongside it and compare transformations or re-specifications where the checks justify them. Explore survey cohort, region controls and leave-one-region-out fits with documented classifications when available. Report cluster counts and sizes and treat any small-cluster results as exploratory.
5. Examine bounded predictions, a fractional-logit mean model with robust uncertainty, the level of WK21, alternate WB_ES measures, influential economies and window changes. Explore national-income confounding using an approved measure only if available and suitable. A survey-only proxy or region adjustment can check a dimension of development without being interpreted as a direct income control. Discuss residual confounding, sampling error and complete-case selection.

## Paper and presentation

Methodology identifies the comparison and inference. Data describes coverage, aggregation and missingness. Results move from the simple fit to the estimates, diagnostics and robustness. Interpretation states the size, precision and limits of the evidence for policymakers, bank credit teams and firms. The conclusion answers each question at the strength the results support.
